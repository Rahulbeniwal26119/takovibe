## What Is Machine Learning?

At its core, **machine learning (ML)** is a branch of **artificial intelligence (AI)** that enables computers to **learn patterns from data**, then **make predictions or decisions** on new data — **without being explicitly programmed** for every scenario.  

In other words: we **train** a model with examples, and then the model **generalizes** those learned patterns to make **inferences** on unseen inputs.

---

## Historical & Conceptual Roots

The term **“machine learning”** emerged from earlier ideas in **statistics** and **artificial intelligence**.\
The goal is **generalization** — the ability of a model to perform well on new data, not just the training examples.  

Early works, like **Arthur Samuel’s checkers-playing program (1959)**, were among the first to show computers that could *improve by themselves* through experience.

## ML in the AI Landscape

It helps to visualize where **Machine Learning** fits within the broader world of AI:

* **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** — the broad domain: any machine that mimics aspects of human intelligence.  
* **Machine Learning (ML)** — a subset of AI where *learning is data-driven*, not hard-coded.  
* **Deep Learning (DL)** — a subset of ML that uses *neural networks* with many layers to learn complex patterns.

## How Machine Learning Works
Let’s look at an abstract pipeline to understand how ML operates:
* Training uses labeled data (in supervised learning) or pattern discovery (in unsupervised learning).
* Inference (prediction) happens when the trained model is used in real-world scenarios.
* Feedback & evaluation let developers measure errors and continuously improve models.

## Types of Machine Learning

Machine Learning is often categorized into three major paradigms:

1. ### Supervised Learning
   * Learns from labeled data (input–output pairs)
   * Common tasks: Regression (predict continuous values) and Classification (categorize data).
   * Example: Predicting house prices or identifying spam emails.
2. ### Unsupervised Learning
   * Works on unlabeled data to discover patterns or structures.
   * Common tasks: Clustering, Anomaly detection, and Dimensionality reduction.
   * Example: Grouping customers by shopping behavior.
3. ### Reinforcement Learning
   * Learns through trial and error, receiving rewards or penalties for actions.
   * Common in robotics, gaming, and autonomous control systems.
   * Example: Training an AI to play chess or drive a car.

Modern AI systems often **combine multiple learning paradigms** — like using **unsupervised pretraining**, **supervised fine-tuning**, and **reinforcement learning** (e.g., RLHF in ChatGPT).

## Why Machine Learning Dominates Modern AI

Why does almost every AI system today use machine learning?

* It’s **nearly impossible to write explicit rules** for complex data — like images, speech, or natural language. Even traditional algorithms such as decision trees eventually reach their limits..
* ML learns from experience, directly from data.
* Modern AI systems **learn to generalize** — adapting to new inputs instead of following static rules.
